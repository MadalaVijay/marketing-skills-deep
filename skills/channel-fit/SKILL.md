# Channel fit

Channels are not good or bad. They fit a business or they do not, and the fit is
mostly determined by four properties of what you sell.

## The four properties that decide

**1. Price point relative to CAC floor.** Every channel has a practical floor
below which you cannot acquire a customer. If the product's contribution margin
is below that floor, the channel cannot work at any level of optimisation.
Paid search on competitive commercial terms has a high floor; content and
community have a high fixed cost but a low marginal one.

**2. Purchase frequency.** One-off purchases must recover CAC immediately.
Repeat purchases can fund a longer payback and therefore a more expensive
channel. This single property rules more channels in or out than any other.

**3. Decision cycle length.** Short cycles suit interruption channels — paid
social, display — because the gap between attention and action is small. Long
cycles need channels that can be re-entered: search, email, community,
retargeting.

**4. Audience concentration.** If buyers gather somewhere identifiable — a
subreddit, a trade body, a conference, a job title on a professional network —
targeted channels beat broad reach. If they are diffuse, broad reach with
strong creative wins.

## Reason from the product, not the trend

The most common error is copying a channel that worked for a company with
different properties. A tactic that works for a $2,000 annual B2B product will
not work for a $9 impulse purchase, and vice versa, regardless of how well it
is executed.

## When a channel is not working

Distinguish three cases before deciding:

| | Evidence | Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Not yet learned** | Under ~30 conversions, under 4 weeks, still in learning | Wait. Judging now is guessing |
| **Executed badly** | Enough volume, but obvious faults: wrong audience, weak creative, broken landing page, no exclusions | Fix, then re-judge. Give it one honest attempt |
| **Structurally unfit** | Fixed the obvious faults, spent enough to know, marginal CAC still above the ceiling | Quit. Redeploy the budget |

Most channels are abandoned in case one and defended in case three.

## The concentration question

Before adding a channel, ask whether the current one has headroom. A second
channel at half the efficiency of the first is worse than more spend on the
first — until the first saturates. Diversify for **risk**, deliberately and with
that named as the reason, or for **headroom**, not for its own sake.

## When to refuse

- Contribution margin or price point unknown
- Fewer than ~30 conversions on the channel being judged
- Asked which channel is "best" without reference to a specific business

## Output

For selection: the two or three channels whose properties match, why, and the
one that fails despite being popular. For a struggling channel: which of the
three cases it is in, the evidence, and either the specific fix or a clear
recommendation to stop.
